**Section label:**

```

**Opening statement:**
```
The East African startup ecosystem doesn't have
an idea problem. It doesn't have a talent problem.

It has a co-execution problem — and an infrastructure
problem that makes the co-execution problem worse
every quarter.
```

**Card 01**
*Label:* `The build gap`
```
Turning a market insight into a working technology
product requires operational depth most founders
don't have and can't afford to hire: product
management, engineering, go-to-market execution,
financial structure.

Every other model — accelerator, incubator, VC —
provides something adjacent to this. None of them
stay in the room and build.
```

**Card 02**
*Label:* `The infrastructure gap`
```
Cloud compute. Development environments. AI-assisted
build tools. In 2026, these are not optional.

For a founder in Dar es Salaam or Nairobi without
external funding, the monthly cost of basic build
infrastructure regularly exceeds $200–500. That is
a month's living expenses.
```

**Card 03**
*Label:* `The access gap`
```
AWS Activate, Google for Startups, Microsoft for
Startups, Anthropic — hundreds of millions in
in-kind credits, available every year. Most East
African founders cannot access them.

Not because they're ineligible. Because these
programs are partnership-gated through accelerator
and VC networks that have no significant presence
on the continent. The design is the problem.
Not the awareness.
```

**Transition statement:**
```
Vuka is built to close all three gaps — for founders,
for the organisations that want to build alongside them,
and for the ecosystem that depends on what gets built.
```

## P2 — THE PROBLEM THIS PATHWAY SOLVES

```
The people who understand a market most deeply are
not always the ones with the co-founding team or the
technical infrastructure to build a company around
what they know.

You have spent years in your sector. You have seen
the gap. You know the customers. You know what the
product needs to do.

What you don't have is a product team, an engineering
function, a go-to-market structure, or a founding

Vuka brings the infrastructure. You bring the domain.
Together, we build the company.

## P3 — HOW IT WORKS

```
[LAYOUT: Four numbered steps. Each: short bold title + 2-sentence description.
Left-border Ember accent on each step.]
```

**Step 01 · Market research and origination**
```
Vuka continuously analyses East African markets across
four sector focus areas. Ideas are generated from pattern
recognition — not waited for.
```

**Step 02 · Internal validation**
```
Before a founding partner is approached, the idea is

**Step 03 · Founding partner identification**
```
Vuka identifies a domain expert — someone with deep
sector knowledge who understands the problem at ground
level. We structure an equity co-founding arrangement
with clear contributions from both sides.

**Step 04 · Build sprint**
```
The venture enters Vuka's 90-day build sprint. Product,
technology, go-to-market, and operational infrastructure
are built simultaneously. The output is a working product —
not a pitch deck.
```

## P2 — THE PROBLEM THIS PATHWAY SOLVES

```
You understand your market. You have talked to customers.
You know what the product needs to do. You may even have
a first version sketched out.

What you don't have is a complete co-founding team — the
product manager, the engineer, the go-to-market lead, the
finance person — all at once, all committed, all available.

Building alone means building slowly, building expensively,
and building without the institutional knowledge that comes
from having done it before. Accelerators offer a programme
and a network. Then they leave. You are back to building
alone, except now you've spent three months in workshops.

Founder-in-Residence puts you inside the studio. Vuka
embeds across your venture — product, technology,
go-to-market, finance — with equity alignment and a
structured 90-day sprint to a working first product.

You don't graduate. The company gets built.
```

---

## P3 — HOW IT WORKS

**Step 01 · Application and selection**
```
You apply with your idea, your market context, and an
honest account of where you are and what you need.
Vuka reviews every application. Selection is based on
market validity, founder-market fit, and sector alignment.
Not on pitch quality.
```

**Step 02 · Scoping and onboarding**
```
A working session to map your idea against Vuka's build
capacity, validate the market hypothesis, and agree on
the equity and operational terms. This takes one to two
weeks. No commitment is required to start this conversation.
```

**Step 03 · 90-day build sprint**
```
You move inside the studio. Product, engineering,
go-to-market, and operational infrastructure are built
simultaneously across a 90-day sprint. The output: a
working product in the hands of real users. Not a prototype.
Not a wireframe. A product.
```

**Step 04 · Post-sprint operational support**
```
Vuka stays. After the sprint, we continue as operational
co-founders — supporting the first sales cycle, the first
hire, the first fundraise. The engagement doesn't end
because the sprint does.
```

# COPY REFERENCE — GLOBAL RULES

*For any writer extending, adapting, or updating this copy.*

| Rule | Applied as |
|---|---|
| Short sentences | Maximum 20 words per sentence in hero and problem sections. Prose sections allow longer — but every paragraph should have at least one sentence under 10 words. |
| "You" over "We" | Count the "you" vs "we" ratio. "You" should appear at least 1.5× as often as "we" across every page. |
| No corporate fluff | Zero instances of: passionate, disruptive, ecosystem play, value-add, synergies, scalable solutions, world-class, transformative, revolutionary, next-generation, empower, leverage, facilitate. |
| Proof over promise | Every capability claim must be followed by a mechanism, named reference, or specific number. Unsubstantiated superlatives do not appear. |
| Stage honesty | Vuka is at founding stage. This is explicit on `/investors`, implied everywhere else through the absence of overclaiming. Portfolio page hidden until first completed venture. |
| East Africa by name | "Dar es Salaam, Nairobi, Kampala" — never "Africa" alone where a specific country or city is accurate. |
| No accelerator language | Cohort, demo day, curriculum, pitch coaching, graduate — none of these appear anywhere in Vuka copy. |
| "Startup speed" always defined | Every instance of "startup speed" in government-facing copy must be followed immediately by a concrete definition: "a working service in 90 days, not an 18-month implementation plan." |
| CTA language | CTAs name the action, not the mechanism. "Start a spinout conversation" not "Contact us." "Apply to Founder-in-Residence" not "Learn more." |
