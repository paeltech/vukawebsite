## The problem

Counterfeit and substandard goods in Tanzania's food and agricultural supply chains were not an awareness problem. Regulators, manufacturers, and consumers all knew the scale of it. The problem was verification — there was no reliable, accessible way to confirm that a product on a shelf or in a field was what the label claimed.

For regulators, enforcement depended on manual inspection at a scale the market had long outgrown. For manufacturers, brand trust eroded with every counterfeit unit in circulation. For consumers, the cost was not abstract.

## The alternatives considered

Traditional approaches fell into three categories — and each left the gap open.

Paper-based certification systems that could not scale to high-frequency commercial activity. Expensive laboratory testing that arrived too late and reached too few verification points. And imported technology platforms built for Western supply chains, not for Tanzania's mix of smartphone penetration, SMS-first communication, and regulatory operating realities.

None of them could deliver traceability at the point of purchase — where the verification actually matters.

## Why they were insufficient

A traceability system that requires a smartphone app excludes the majority of retail verification moments. A system that requires laboratory infrastructure excludes regulators operating at national scale. A system built for a single stakeholder — manufacturer or regulator alone — does not survive the commercial incentives of the market it enters.

The gap required a dual-channel architecture: accessible to consumers without smartphones, credible to regulators with enforcement authority, and commercially viable for manufacturers with distribution relationships to protect.

## What Vuka built

Hakiki is a dual-channel product traceability and anti-counterfeit platform — built for the Tanzanian market from the ground up, not adapted from a template developed elsewhere.

SMS-first verification for consumers. A regulatory interface for TOSCI and sector authorities. Manufacturer onboarding for brands with distribution networks to protect. The system was designed for East African operating realities: unreliable connectivity, mixed digital literacy, and the regulatory relationships required to make traceability enforceable — not just visible.

## The outcome

Hakiki was deployed with TOSCI, ASA, TTCL, and AATF — spanning regulatory, agricultural, and telecommunications stakeholders across Tanzania's food and agricultural supply chain.

The platform was recognised at the presidential level in May 2019. It is operational, established, and still running — not a pilot that required a second procurement cycle to scale.
