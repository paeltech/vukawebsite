# Vuka Brand Direction
### Visual Identity & Colour System · May 2026

---

## Brand Philosophy

Vuka's visual identity is built around a single principle: **rootedness without nostalgia**.

The brand must be legible to a DFI investment officer in Geneva, a first-time founder in Arusha, and a corporate innovation lead in Nairobi — on the same day, in the same materials. It cannot look like a Western VC that has added African colours as an afterthought. And it cannot lean into a romanticised visual language of the continent that signals tourism rather than industry.

The solution is a palette drawn entirely from the physical landscape and materials of East Africa — soil layers, baobab bark, volcanic rift geology, dried savanna grass, old parchment — rendered with the restraint and precision of a modern studio brand. Warm, grounded, serious, and unmistakably of this place.

## Colour Rationale

### Vuka Ember `#C4581C`
The heat of action — fire, urgency, forward motion. Mirrors the burnt-orange in the logo mark. In Zulu and Xhosa, *vuka* is a command. This colour carries that energy: it does not sit quietly. It is the colour that stops the eye on a page, anchors a call-to-action, and gives the brand its single point of intensity. Used sparingly, it earns its authority.

### Vuka Parchment `#F5EFE6`
Warm, aged paper — the material of founding documents, manifestos, and ideas that last. Not clinical white. Deliberately warm, rooted, and human. Sets Vuka apart from the cold tech-blue and stark-white defaults that dominate the African innovation ecosystem. Every layout breathes against this background.

### Vuka Black `#1A1008`
Not pure black. A near-black with warm undertones — soil at depth, dark hardwood, rich earth. Every text element in the system sits in this colour. It ties the typography into the warm palette rather than floating above it. The difference between `#000000` and `#1A1008` is invisible at a glance and decisive in feel.

### Vuka Deep Soil `#3D2B0E`
The dark layer beneath the surface — where roots grow and foundations are set. Used for dark-mode backgrounds, full-bleed pitch deck slides, and section dividers where depth and gravitas are needed. Warmer and richer than a standard dark navy; it reads as serious without being corporate.

### Vuka Baobab `#8B5A2B`
The baobab tree is one of the oldest living things on the continent — patient, resilient, structurally extraordinary. This mid-brown bridges Ember and Deep Soil, functioning as a secondary accent in data visualisations, chart series, and supporting UI elements. It is the colour that holds the system together when Ember is too loud and Deep Soil too dark.

### Vuka Sand `#D4C4A8`
Understated. The colour of dry savanna grass and coastal dunes. Holds the palette together without demanding attention. Ideal for muted UI elements, disabled states, and the subtle borders that give structure to layouts without adding visual noise. The palette's exhale.

### Vuka Rift Green `#2A4A3A`
Named for the East African Rift — the most dramatic geological feature in the region, and the source of some of its most fertile land. This deep forest green anchors all climate, energy, and environmental sector work. Cool, grounded, and unmistakably East African without being generic. It is the one colour in the system that leans cool — a deliberate counterpoint to the warmth of the rest of the palette. In data visualisations and sector-tagged content, it signals: this is the work that connects capital to land.

## Usage Rules

### The Three Core Combinations

**Default (all printed and digital materials)**
- Background: Vuka Parchment `#F5EFE6`
- Text: Vuka Black `#1A1008`
- Accent: Vuka Ember `#C4581C`

**Bold / Statement (social headers, event covers, campaign materials)**
- Background: Vuka Ember `#C4581C`
- Text & logo: Vuka Parchment `#F5EFE6`

**Dark / Prestige (pitch decks, DFI presentations, formal documents)**
- Background: Vuka Deep Soil `#3D2B0E`
- Text: Vuka Parchment `#F5EFE6`
- Accent: Vuka Ember `#C4581C`

### Tags, Badges, and Programme Labels
- Sector tags: Ember fill with Parchment text; Rift Green fill with Sand text
- Programme badges: Black fill with Parchment text, set in DM Mono (monospace)
- Status indicators: Sand fill with Baobab text

### Data and Charts
The colour hierarchy for data series follows the warmth gradient of the landscape:
1. Vuka Ember `#C4581C` — primary series
2. Vuka Baobab `#8B5A2B` — secondary series
3. Vuka Rift Green `#2A4A3A` — tertiary / climate-specific series
4. Vuka Sand `#D4C4A8` — baseline / comparison series

### What to Avoid
- Do not use pure white (`#FFFFFF`) as a background. Parchment is the white.
- Do not use pure black (`#000000`) for text. Vuka Black is the black.
- Do not introduce colours from outside this system. The palette's coherence is its strength.
- Do not place Ember on Deep Soil without sufficient contrast testing — the combination reads warm but can reduce legibility at small sizes.

## Typography Direction

Vuka's typographic system is built entirely on **Codec Pro**.

Codec Pro is a geometric sans-serif with a confident, clean character — modern without being cold, structured without being rigid. It carries the same qualities the studio stands for: precision, directness, and a refusal to default to convention. The typeface works across the full weight range from thin editorial headings to bold display statements, meaning the entire brand expression lives within a single, coherent type family rather than a patchwork of paired fonts.

### Weight Usage

| Weight | Application |
|---|---|
| **Codec Pro ExtraBold / Heavy** | Wordmark · Hero headings · Section titles |
| **Codec Pro Bold** | Subheadings · Pull quotes · Callout text |
| **Codec Pro Regular** | Body copy · Paragraph text · Descriptions |
| **Codec Pro Light / Thin** | Captions · Labels · Supporting metadata |

### Typographic Principles

- The wordmark is always set in Codec Pro Heavy, lowercase: **vuka** — the weight gives it authority without needing capitalisation.
- Headlines are set in Codec Pro ExtraBold or Heavy, with tight letter-spacing (`-0.02em` to `-0.04em`) for impact at large sizes.
- Body text is set in Codec Pro Regular at comfortable reading sizes (16–18px digital, 10–11pt print), with generous line-height (1.65–1.75) to match the openness of the Parchment background.
- Labels, programme identifiers, hex references, and metadata are set in Codec Pro Light or Thin with tracked letter-spacing (`+0.12em` to `+0.18em`) — the contrast between the heavy display weights and the open tracked light weights creates the system's typographic rhythm.
- All-caps is used sparingly and only in Codec Pro Light with wide tracking, for category labels and section markers.

### What to Avoid

- Do not mix Codec Pro with other typefaces. The system is intentionally single-family.
- Do not use Codec Pro Italic as a default — reserve it for specific editorial emphasis within long-form content only.
- Do not set body copy in weights heavier than Regular. The warmth of the Parchment background requires text that breathes.

## The Name in the Brand

The wordmark is always lowercase: **vuka**

The full-stop period after the name — **vuka.** — is set in Vuka Ember. It is not decorative punctuation. It is a deliberate signal: the name is a sentence. An instruction. A full stop that is also an ignition.
